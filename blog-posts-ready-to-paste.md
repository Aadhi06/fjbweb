<h2>The Simple Formula</h2>
<p>Most gold rings are valued as: <strong>Weight (grams) × Purity × Live gold price per gram = Value</strong>. Buyers then apply a small margin for refining and business costs — this is the "We Pay" price you see on our calculator.</p>

<h2>Step 1: Weigh Your Ring</h2>
<p>Use a kitchen or jeweller's scale in <strong>grams</strong>. A typical ladies' 9ct gold ring might weigh 2–5g; a men's 18ct band might be 8–15g. Even small differences in weight affect the final offer.</p>

<h2>Step 2: Identify the Carat</h2>
<p>Check inside the band for a hallmark:</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>375</strong> = 9ct gold</li>
  <li><strong>750</strong> = 18ct gold</li>
  <li><strong>916</strong> = 22ct gold</li>
</ul>
<p>No hallmark? We can test it in-store — <a href="/book-appointment">book a free valuation</a>.</p>

<h2>Step 3: Check Live Gold Prices</h2>
<p>Gold prices change daily. Our <a href="/live-rates">Live Rates</a> page shows current market and buying prices per gram for each carat. Our <a href="/gold-calculator">Gold Calculator</a> updates every 30 seconds so you always see a current estimate.</p>

<h2>Rings With Diamonds or Gemstones</h2>
<p>If your ring has a diamond or gemstone, the stone may add significant value beyond the gold weight. Do not remove stones yourself — bring the complete ring for assessment. Our gemologists can value diamonds and coloured stones separately.</p>

<h2>Branded &amp; Designer Rings</h2>
<p>Rings from Cartier, Tiffany, Van Cleef, Boodles, and similar houses may command a premium over scrap value. We buy branded jewellery and assess collector or resale value where applicable.</p>

<h2>Online Estimate vs In-Shop Offer</h2>
<p>Our online calculator gives a strong estimate for gold weight and purity. <p>Many people assume broken gold jewellery is worthless. It is not. Gold buyers purchase <strong>scrap gold</strong> every day — snapped chains, bent rings, single earrings, and tangled knots all contain valuable metal that is recycled and refined.</p>

<h2>What Counts as "Broken Gold"?</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Snapped or kinked chains and bracelets</li>
  <li>Rings that no longer fit or have damaged settings</li>
  <li>Single earrings (lost their pair)</li>
  <li>Gold watches that no longer work (we buy these too)</li>
  <li>Inherited jewellery you will never wear</li>
  <li>Any gold marked 375, 750, 916, or similar</li>
</ul>

<h2>How Broken Gold Is Priced</h2>
<p>Appearance does not matter — <strong>weight and carat</strong> do. A broken 18ct chain weighs the same as a perfect one, so the gold value is identical. We weigh your items, test purity, and offer a price based on live market rates. See today's prices on our <a href="/live-rates">Live Rates</a> page.</p>

<h2>Should You Repair or Sell?</h2>
<p>Repair costs often exceed the scrap value of low-carat pieces. For 9ct fashion jewellery, selling for gold value is frequently the smarter choice. For antique, branded, or high-carat items, visit us first — repair or resale may be worth more than scrap.</p>

<h2>Tips Before You Sell</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Do not clean aggressively</strong> — harsh chemicals are unnecessary</li>
  <li><strong>Separate by carat</strong> if you can identify hallmarks</li>
  <li><strong>Bring everything</strong> — even small scraps add up</li>
  <li><strong>Remove non-gold attachments</strong> only if you are certain they are not gold</li>
</ul>
